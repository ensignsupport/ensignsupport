# Photography

downloaded and served locally rather than hot-linked, so the site has no runtime
dependency on a third party and nothing about a visitor reaches Unsplash.

## Why these two, and why not more

The design direction document is specific about this, and it was right: free
stock libraries have thin and often stereotyped disability collections. Searching
them returns empty wheelchairs photographed as objects, greyscale melancholy,
silhouettes, and people shot from behind or from above. Those read as pitying to
the exact audience this site is for.

**`community.jpg`** is the exception that survived the brief: a man on his own
high street, at eye level, unaccompanied, in ordinary clothes, doing an ordinary
thing, with nobody helping him in frame. It comes from the Centre for Ageing
Better's collection, which exists specifically to counter stereotyped portrayals.
It is a UK street rather than an Australian one, which is a compromise the
direction document anticipated but did not prefer.

**`plan-desk.jpg`** is the safety valve the document recommends: documents and a
pen in daylight, no faces, no representation risk at all.

## What is still missing

No photograph of the operator. That remains the single highest-value asset
available to a solo practice and no stock image substitutes for it.

Commissioned photography of real participants, with consent, once the practice
has been running a few months, will beat both of these. Plan for the swap.

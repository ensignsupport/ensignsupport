What each score means
  • Clear in 5 seconds: can you tell what they do, for whom, and where?
  • Information order: does the page answer questions in the order visitors ask them (from the audience research)?
  • Trust: real named people, proof, credentials, independence.
  • Easy to contact or refer: phone, button or form reachable without hunting, nothing blocking.
  • Reading load: words, text size, walls of text. Higher is lighter.
  • Visual craft: does it look designed on purpose, or like a template?
  • Mobile first screen: what a phone user sees before scrolling.
  • Accessibility basics: text size, headings, pop-ups, contrast seen on screen. Not a full automated audit (see limits).

Patterns

What everyone does (we avoid)

  • Open with a paragraph wall about "your NDIS journey"
  • Stock smiling photos, no named person
  • Vague words: empower, person centred, high quality
  • Never say who you will speak to, how fast they reply, or which plan types they take
  • Pop-ups and banners on arrival

What works (we keep)

  • Split visitors in one tap: participant, family, referrer (Leap in!, Hireup)
  • A named real person with a face (Mosh, CaRelief)
  • A 3-step "how it starts" (Mosh, Your Kind)
  • One short promise plus one clear button (Hireup, Calm)
  • Big readable text and generous space (Calm)

What nobody does (Ensign's opening)

  • Answer the questions the NDIS tells people to ask, on the first screens: who you speak to, reply time, plan types, area, cost, what is not offered
  • A fast lane for referrers: refer in under 2 minutes
  • Andrew himself as the proof: name, face, 7+ years, degrees, Tamil and English
  • Plain language built for cognitive and psychosocial disability
  • Independence stated as a strength (research: independent coordinators are trusted more)

House pattern: Mighty Moose Mowing

Not scored. Used as Archway's reference for laying out information. open site

  • Proof first: Google 5.0 rating sits above the headline
  • Short, confident headline: 4 words
  • Show, don't tell: drag-to-compare before and after on the first screen
  • Action on the first screen: phone, hours and who picks up ("Johnross picks up"), plus a short tick-box form
  • Trust strip: insured, since 2014, quoted in person, no lock-in
  • Real reviews, word for word
  • Sticky mobile bar: Call and Free quote always one tap away
  • For Ensign: no reviews or before and after exist, so the proof slot becomes Andrew (face, name, years, degrees) and the plain promises (reply within 1 business day, 7am to 7pm, seven days)

Limits of this benchmark

  • Automated accessibility and speed tools could not reach these sites from our cloud workspace (blocked by network policy). Accessibility scores are from what is visible, not an automated audit.
  • Screenshots were viewed live in the browser but could not be saved into this file. Use the "open site" links.
  • Breakthru's Support Coordination page no longer exists. Scored on the page the client's link now lands on.
  • Calm blocked the mobile view with a bot check, so mobile is not scored and its total is scaled from 7 criteria.
  • One reviewer scored this (me). Phase 2 Step 7 sends it to a blind reviewer.

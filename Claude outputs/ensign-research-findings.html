Ensign Support Coordination: Research Findings

Phase 2 output. Status: DRAFT (17 Sep 2026, decisions added). Locks when Allen says “lock”. Input: phase-1/ensign-facts.md (LOCKED 16 Sep 2026). For Phase 3 to write content and design the website.

1. Summary for Phase 3

  • Andrew is taking new participants now. Getting new clients is the site’s main goal.
  • Visitors are participants (many with psychosocial or cognitive disability), families and carers, and professional referrers. Each asks practical questions first: what it is, who it’s for, area, plan types, who they will speak to, how fast they get a reply, how to refer.
  • The top complaint about support coordinators is not replying. Andrew’s “reply within 1 business day” answers it directly.
  • Trust comes from a named person you will actually speak to, independence and clear limits. Ensign has no testimonials, so Andrew himself (photo, 7+ years, two social work degrees, English and Tamil) is the proof.
  • Local competitors are weak. None answers the practical questions early, and none shows a real named coordinator well. One of Andrew’s named references (Hively Coordination) says it is closed for new referrals and exiting support coordination through 2026 to 2027. Breakthru has no support coordination page any more.
  • The quality bar is set by premium health sites (Mosh) and the best national NDIS sites (Hireup, Leap in!), not local competitors.
  • Allen’s chosen reference is Therapy in London: big type at regular weight, one accent colour, lots of space, few words, a task first, and a hand-drawn scribble that gives “mild perfect imperfection”.
  • Colour: indigo scale from Andrew’s pigment photo, warm neutrals, all shipping pairs pass WCAG 2.2 AA. The pure pigment indigo cannot be text in dark mode.
  • Fonts: the setting matters more than the name. Shortlist: Figtree, GT America, ABC Diatype Rounded, with Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for long reading. Final pick in Phase 3.
  • House patterns to carry in: conversational enquiry that opens Messages on phone and sends by Resend on computer; owner portrait in a hand-made frame; visuals over text for steps and key facts; a spacing system before any section is designed.
  • Design drafts A1 v1 to v6 exist from before Phase 2’s scope was narrowed. They are Phase 3 inputs to learn from, not locked designs.

2. Audience findings

Full detail and sources: research-audiences.md.

Participants (including psychosocial disability)

Questions, in order: 1. What does a support coordinator or recovery coach actually do for me? 2. Do I need one, the other, or both? 3. Do they work with people with my disability? 4. Do they have lived experience (recovery coaching)? 5. Will I get along with them? 6. Who will I actually speak to? 7. What does it cost, and what is the cancellation policy? 8. What are their qualifications? 9. Are they registered, and does it matter for me? 10. Are they available at times and places that suit me? 11. Will they come to meetings with me?

Trust builders: a named person, lived experience stated openly (recovery coaching), a trauma-informed tone, being invited to talk to more than one coach. Trust killers: being overridden, coercion or boundary crossing, refusing to attend meetings.

Families, carers and nominees

Questions, in order: Is our current coordinator doing the job? Can we change mid plan? Where do you find good ones? Will they contact us the way we ask? Are they independent? Do they know plan reviews well? What have they achieved for people like ours? How will they respect both the nominee and the participant? Who do we complain to?

Trust builders: independence, the preferred contact channel respected, transparent pricing and cancellation terms. Trust killers: not replying (“repeated emails and phone calls for four months”), overcharging, plan review mistakes.

Professional referrers (LACs, hospital social workers, plan managers, GPs, allied health)

Questions, in order (mostly inference, no Australian survey found): Do you have capacity now? Do you cover this area? Which plan types? Do you work with this cohort? How do I refer and what do you need? How fast will you contact the participant and confirm back? Can you work to a discharge timeline? Are you independent? Qualifications, screening, insurance? Will you keep me updated within consent?

Accessibility and reading needs

Plain, literal language. Clear page purpose and short paths to key tasks. Forms that don’t rely on memory, visible labels, no timeouts. Human help and other ways to make contact. Easy Read level guidance: year 4 to 6 reading level, 14pt minimum, 1.5 line spacing. Calm tone and imagery, no pressure.

Thin evidence

Referrer decision making (inference). Forum quotes are thread titles and snippets. No Google review analysis. Two peer-reviewed studies not accessible.

Assumption challenged

Being unregistered is not a weakness to hide behind warm language. The NDIS treats registration as a neutral question, and independent coordinators are trusted more. (Allen’s decision: registration status stays in the footer. Phase 3 may state independence in the body.)

3. Market findings

Full detail: benchmark.html.

Scores (two independent rounds)

Site Type Round 1 (visual, desktop and phone) Round 2 (blind, text only, 6 criteria scaled)

Hireup National NDIS 33 Not reachable
Calm Premium wellbeing 31 29
Leap in! National NDIS (plan management) 30 27
Breakthru Local, Andrew’s reference 24 23
Your Kind Collective Local 21 29
Hively Coordination Local, Andrew’s reference 19 29
CaRelief Local, Andrew’s reference 19 25

What the two rounds agree on: - Mosh is the strongest in both. It is the quality bar. - No local competitor scores well on trust (both rounds give trust 2 or 3). None shows a real named coordinator with credentials. - The rounds disagree on local reading load and craft, because round 2 could not see the pages (text only, no visual or phone scores). Treat local scores as a range, roughly 19 to 29.

Market facts found: - Hively Coordination: “Hively Coordination will be gradually exiting Support Coordination services throughout 2026 - 2027”, shown after a note that it is closed to new support coordination referrals (page text, 17 Sep 2026). - Breakthru: the support coordination page Andrew named now redirects to a general services page. - CaRelief: opens with a pop-up ad, no phone link on the page. - Local fonts: Poppins, Lato, Quicksand, Nunito (all common free fonts).

Patterns

  • What everyone does (avoid): a paragraph wall about “your NDIS journey”, stock photos with no named person, vague words (empower, person centred), never saying who you speak to, reply time or plan types, pop-ups on arrival.
  • What works (keep): splitting visitors in one tap (Leap in!, Hireup), a named real person with a face (Mosh), a short “how it starts” (Mosh, Your Kind), one short promise and one clear action (Hireup, Calm), big readable text and space (Calm).
  • What nobody does (Ensign’s opening): answering the NDIS’s own recommended questions on the first screens; a fast lane for referrers; the coordinator himself as the proof; plain language built for cognitive and psychosocial disability; stating limits honestly (what is not offered).

4. Parameters

Full detail: parameters.md. In short: - Answer practical questions first, low reading load, calm, real named person, fast referrer path. - Indigo, mild and warm. Five pages. Andrew’s real photo. No invented proof. Footer states unregistered. - WCAG 2.2 AA, facts only, copy and claims rules, privacy policy for the referral form. - Style preset: calm layout (variance 3 to 4), light motion (2 to 3), few words per screen (density 4 to 5). Avoid the named AI tells.

5. Inspiration

Full detail: reference-board.html (18 award-recognised sites) and reference-analysis-therapyin-london.md.

Allen’s pick: Therapy in London (https://www.therapyin.london/).

Allen’s words: “I like the placement, layout. There is a mild perfect imperfection to it which gives the human touch. It’s perfect but there is a mild imperfect feel. The scribble gives character.”

Measured: one font (Figtree) for everything; headings at regular weight, very large with tight letter spacing (128px, -5px); section headings 40px; warm stone background #EDEAE1; soft ink text #2A2A2A; one accent #3881F1; 30px corners; about 430 words over 12 screens; first screen is a task (“What do you need help with?”, “Where are you based?”); real named therapists with personal profiles.

Direction principles: - Precise, restrained layout plus one hand-made human mark per screen. - Big type at regular weight, deliberate line breaks, lots of space, one accent colour. - Task first instead of a sales pitch. - Real named person with personal detail. - Very few words per screen.

Also noted by Allen during drafts (carry into Phase 3): - Conversational enquiry that does not feel like a form, opening the phone’s Messages app pre-filled, and sending by Resend on a computer (C23). - The owner’s photo in a hand-made brush stroke frame with a tilted name card, never a plain square (C24). - Enquiry choices styled as part of the page with calm, satisfying interaction (C25, C26). - Spacing and layout system first, one job per section (C29). - Steps and key facts shown visually (C30).

Only one reference was picked. Maxima Therapy (warmer) and Twofold Therapy (solo practitioner) were used as secondary references with Allen’s agreement.

6. Colour findings

Full detail: colour-sheet.html, ensign-palette.json. - Indigo scale built from the pigment photo core #193abd: 50 #f3f7ff, 100 #e5edff, 200 #cadbff, 300 #a0bdfe, 400 #6993fa, 500 #416ce5, 600 #2850d3, 700 #2041a8, 800 #173182, 900 #0f225e, 950 #061237. - Warm neutrals: 0 #fffdfa, 50 #faf6f1, 100 #f2eee7, 200 #e2ddd7, 400 #a29e98, 600 #6d6861, 800 #39352f, 900 #231f19. - Light mode: warm page, near-black warm text, indigo-600 buttons and links, indigo-900 headings. Dark mode: indigo-950 page, indigo-50 text, indigo-300 buttons and links. - All shipping pairs pass WCAG 2.2 AA (5.1:1 to 17:1). Rule: the pure pigment indigo is never text in dark mode (2.77:1). - Allen’s preferred strength: indigo accents on a warm page (A1), not large dark indigo areas.

7. Type findings

Full detail: type-findings.md. - Shortlist: Figtree (free); GT America (Grilli Type, paid); ABC Diatype Rounded (Dinamo, paid); Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for long reading (free). - Ruled out: Outfit and Manrope, Inter by default, Fraunces, Instrument Serif, script or decorative fonts for text, Century Gothic for body text. - The setting (size, weight, spacing, restraint) matters more than the font name. - Final choice, testing inside real Ensign sections, blind review and licence check happen in Phase 3.

8. Page question maps

Home

  • Arrives: all three groups, from Google, a referral link or a word of mouth search.
  • Questions in order: What is this? Is it for me (or the person I support)? Do you cover my area? Who will I speak to? How fast will I hear back? How do I start?
  • Facts that answer: two services; all ages, all disability types; self managed and plan managed; the 9 areas; Andrew Manoba; reply within 1 business day, 7am to 7pm, seven days; enquiry flow.
  • Capacity: taking new participants now (Allen, 17 Sep). Say so plainly on the first screen.
  • Cost: PLACEHOLDER wording, marked, until Allen confirms.
  • One action: start the enquiry (who, where, details).

Services

  • Arrives: participants and families comparing options, referrers checking fit.
  • Questions in order: What does support coordination do? What does recovery coaching do? Which do I need, or both? What does a normal week look like? What is not offered? Which plan types? In person or by phone?
  • Facts that answer: both services; week to week description (PLACEHOLDER, Andrew to sign off); not offered list; plan types; in person visits; recovery coaching can be by phone; written summary before each plan review; bills actual time; itemised invoices.
  • Gaps: the real week to week description from Andrew; lived experience (not stated, do not imply).
  • One action: start the enquiry for the chosen service.

About (Andrew)

  • Arrives: anyone deciding whether to trust Andrew.
  • Questions in order: Who is he? What’s his experience? Qualifications? Will I get along with him? Is he independent? What does he stand for?
  • Facts that answer: name and photo; 7+ years; Support Coordinator, Social Worker, Disability Clinician; Bachelor of Social Work, Master of Social Work (UQ); screening checks; English and Tamil; CALD trained; values Compassion, Integrity, Advocacy (avoid presenting as a triad).
  • Gaps: Andrew’s own words on why he does this work (optional, ask at showcase).
  • One action: talk to Andrew.

Referral

  • Arrives: professional referrers and families with documents.
  • Questions in order: Can you take this participant (area, plan type, cohort)? What do you need from me? How long will it take? What happens after I submit? Is the information kept safe?
  • Facts that answer: areas; plan types; referral form fields and uploads; reply within 1 business day with a reference number; first meeting within the first week; privacy policy and collection notice.
  • Capacity: taking new referrals now (Allen, 17 Sep).
  • Gaps: where uploaded files are stored (Phase 4).
  • One action: submit the referral form.

Contact

  • Arrives: anyone wanting to talk first.
  • Questions in order: How do I reach Andrew? When is he available? How fast will he reply? Can I get an interpreter?
  • Facts that answer: phone and email (PLACEHOLDER); 7am to 7pm, seven days; reply within 1 business day; interpreters arranged; enquiry form; not offered: crisis or after hours support.
  • Crisis signpost (Allen, 17 Sep): a clear note that Ensign is not a crisis service, pointing to Lifeline 13 11 14 (24 hours) and 000 in an emergency. Check both numbers again at go live.
  • One action: send an enquiry or call.
